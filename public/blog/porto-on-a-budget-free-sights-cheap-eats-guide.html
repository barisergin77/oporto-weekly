Baris here, editor of Oporto Weekly. A lot of what makes Porto worth the trip costs nothing: the streets of the old centre, the bridge, the viewpoints, the tiles. Food is cheap if you eat where locals eat, and the transport system rewards a little planning. This guide covers the free sights, the places I send people for a cheap lunch, and the tickets and passes that actually save money.

Free Things to See in Porto

The historic centre is compact and best seen on foot. Wear proper shoes, because it is steep and the cobbles are slippery when wet.

The Old Centre, the Bridge and the Station

Start in the Ribeira, the riverside quarter inside the UNESCO-listed historic centre. Walk the quay, then get lost in the lanes behind it.

From there, walk across the upper deck of the Dom Luís I Bridge. It is free, the metro runs across it next to you, and the view over the Ribeira and the port wine lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia is the best photo spot in the city.

A short walk away is São Bento station. Step into the main hall, even if you are not catching a train: the walls are covered in blue and white azulejo panels (around 20,000 tiles) showing scenes from Portuguese history.

Up the hill, the terrace in front of the Sé (the cathedral, Terreiro da Sé) gives you a free view over the old town and is a good place to get your bearings. Other free stops nearby: the tiled façade of the Capela das Almas on Rua de Santa Catarina, the tiled Igreja de Santo Ildefonso near Praça da Batalha, and the long sweep of Avenida dos Aliados up to the City Hall.

Viewpoints and Gardens

  • Jardins do Palácio de Cristal: gardens with river views and free-roaming peacocks. Free entry. Opening hours in winter are 8:00 to 19:00, later in summer. Where: Rua de Dom Manuel II, 4050-346 Porto.
  • Jardim do Morro: the hilltop garden at the Gaia end of the bridge. This is where people gather to watch the sun go down behind the Ribeira. Just above it, the Miradouro da Serra do Pilar gives you a slightly higher view.
  • Miradouro da Vitória: a small viewpoint in the old Jewish quarter, looking over the rooftops to the river and the Sé. Where: Rua de São Bento da Vitória, Porto.
  • Parque da Cidade: the big city park out towards the sea, free to enter, with lakes, lawns and paths that bring you out at the seafront.

Free and Cheap Culture

  • Centro Português de Fotografia: photography exhibitions in the old Relação prison and courthouse. Entry is free. When: exhibitions Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00. Where: Largo Amor de Perdição, 4050-008 Porto. Details on cpf.pt.
  • Mercado do Bolhão: walking around the restored market costs nothing. When: Monday to Friday 8:00 to 19:00, Saturday 8:00 to 18:00, closed Sundays and public holidays. Where: Rua Formosa 322, Porto.
  • Serralves: the free first Sunday of the month is only for residents of Portugal (with a Portuguese ID or residence card). Children under 12 always get in free. Everyone else pays: €15 for the park only, €24 for all spaces. Check serralves.pt before you go.
  • Museu Nacional Soares dos Reis: the general ticket is €10. Portuguese citizens and residents get in free on any day, up to 52 visits a year. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00. Where: Rua de D. Manuel II 44.
  • Torre dos Clérigos at night: the tower-only night ticket is €5 (19:00 to 23:00), but it only runs at Easter, in summer and over Christmas. The day ticket for tower and museum is €10. See torredosclerigos.pt.
  • Livraria Lello: entry is a €15.95 ticket-voucher, which you can deduct from a book from Edições Livraria Lello. If you were going to buy a book anyway, the visit costs you nothing extra. Open daily, 9:00 to 19:30. Tickets on tickets.livrarialello.pt.

Cheap Eats in Porto

Porto's best-known dishes are also among its cheapest. The bifana is a pork sandwich, thin slices of marinated pork in a soft roll. The francesinha is the heavy one: bread, steak, ham and sausage under melted cheese and a beer and tomato sauce, usually with chips. At lunchtime, look for a prato do dia (dish of the day) chalked up outside a local tasca. It is usually the cheapest proper meal in town.

Where I Send People

  • Conga: bifanas, draught beer, no fuss. Where: Rua do Bonjardim 318, 4000-115 Porto. When: Monday to Saturday, 11:30 to 22:00; closed Sundays.
  • Casa Guedes: known for its roast pork leg sandwich (sandes de pernil), with or without Serra da Estrela cheese. Where: Praça dos Poveiros 130, 4000-393 Porto.
  • Café Santiago: a classic for francesinha. Expect a queue at peak times. Where: Rua de Passos Manuel 226, 4000-382 Porto. When: Monday to Saturday, 12:00 to 22:45; closed Sundays.
  • Cervejaria Gazela: the place for cachorrinhos, small crusty hot dogs with cheese and a spicy sauce. They are small, so order a few. Where: Travessa do Cimo de Vila 4, 4000-434 Porto. When: Monday to Saturday, 12:00 to 22:30; closed Sundays.
  • Mercado do Bolhão: the restaurants and stalls on the upper floor stay open until midnight, Monday to Saturday, and are good for grazing on small plates.

One habit that saves money every day: drink your coffee standing at the counter. A cimbalino (espresso) at the bar costs less than the same coffee at a table, especially in the tourist areas.

Money-Saving Tips

Getting Around

Walk when you can. For longer trips, the metro, buses and CP urban trains all take the Andante card. Fares from 1 January 2026, from andante.pt:

  • Andante Azul: the paper card costs €0.60 and you top it up with trips. A single Z2 trip (most of central Porto) is €1.40 and lasts an hour from first validation, changes included. Buy 10 single trips and you get one free.
  • Andante 24: 24 hours of unlimited travel, €5.35 for Z2. Wider zones cost more.
  • Andante Tour: unlimited travel on the whole Andante network, €7.75 for 1 day or €16.55 for 3 days. It is not valid on the historic trams or the Guindais funicular.
  • Validate every time you board or change line, on the metro and train at the station, on the bus at the door.

The metro (line E) is the cheapest way in from the airport. For the coast, STCP bus 500 runs out to Foz. The historic trams (lines 1, 18 and 22) are run by STCP and you pay the conductor on board; they are a ride for the views, not a cheap way to get around.

City Cards and Discounts

When I checked, the Porto Card was off sale while it is being restructured. Check visitporto.travel before you plan around it. Many sights give discounts to students and over-65s (Serralves gives 50% off to both), so carry ID.

When to Come

Spring (April and May) and autumn (September and October) usually mean good weather and lower prices than summer. Winter, November to February, is cheaper still if you don't mind rain.

Last tip: Porto's tap water is safe to drink, so bring a refillable bottle.
