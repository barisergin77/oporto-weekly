Hello fellow Porto lovers,

It’s Baris here, from Oporto Weekly. One of the questions I get asked most often by visitors and newcomers alike is, "Where can I find something truly special to take home?" While the city is famous for its Port wine and beautifully tiled façades, there’s a whole other world of shopping here that goes far beyond the typical souvenir shop. I’m talking about a vibrant, breathing ecosystem of markets, vintage boutiques, and artisan workshops where the city's creative soul truly shines.

Forget the mass-produced trinkets. Today, I want to take you on a personal tour of my favourite spots to unearth unique treasures. This is my guide to shopping like a local in Porto, whether you’re hunting for a one-of-a-kind vintage jacket, a piece of art from an emerging designer, or simply the thrill of a flea market bargain. Let’s dive in.

The Soul of the City: Porto's Vibrant Markets

There’s no better way to feel the pulse of Porto than by wandering through one of its bustling markets. The air buzzes with chatter, the scent of fresh flowers and grilled sardines hangs in the air, and every stall holds a potential discovery. Each market has its own distinct personality, so you can easily find one that suits your style.

The Iconic: Mercado do Bolhão

If you only have time for one market, make it the Mercado do Bolhão. After a magnificent restoration, this two-story iron landmark is more beautiful than ever. While the ground floor is a feast for the senses with vendors selling fresh produce, glistening fish, and local cheeses, don’t miss the stalls offering lovely handicrafts. When you need a break, head to the upper level, where the food stalls and restaurants make it easy to stop for lunch.

  • Address: Rua Formosa, Baixa (side entrances on Rua de Sá da Bandeira and Rua Alexandre Braga)
  • Hours: Monday to Friday 8:00 to 20:00, Saturday 8:00 to 18:00. Closed Sundays and public holidays (mercadobolhao.pt).

The Treasure Hunt: Feira da Vandoma

For the true bargain hunters and lovers of the delightfully unexpected, the Feira da Vandoma is an absolute must. Every Saturday morning, this sprawling flea market transforms Avenida 25 de Abril into a paradise of pre-loved goods. You can find everything here: vinyl records, quirky furniture, antique tools, piles of clothes, and boxes of old books. My advice? Get there early, as the best items are snapped up quickly, and many vendors start packing up around 1:00 PM. And remember, bargaining is part of the fun, so have your cash ready and don’t be shy!

  • Address: Avenida 25 de Abril, Campanhã
  • Hours: Saturdays, 8:00 to 13:00.

The Curated Cool: Mercado Porto Belo

Inspired by London’s famous Portobello Market, this Saturday gathering at Praça de Carlos Alberto offers a more curated and artisanal experience. It’s the perfect place to find high-quality vintage clothing, handmade jewellery from local designers, organic products, and a fantastic selection of vinyl records. The vibe is relaxed and creative, making it a wonderful spot to spend a Saturday afternoon, browsing the stalls and soaking in the atmosphere.

  • Address: Praça de Carlos Alberto, Porto
  • Hours: Saturdays. Hours change with the season, so check Agenda Porto before you go.

The Modern Pop-Up: Urban Market

If your taste leans more contemporary, keep an eye out for the Urban Market. This pop-up event showcases the work of Porto’s independent designers and artists, offering everything from cutting-edge fashion to unique home decor. Its location is fluid—past editions have been at Praça das Cardosas, Praça da Alegria and indoors at the Hard Club. Your best bet is to check their social media channels for the latest dates and venues before you head out.

A Trip Back in Time: Porto’s Best Vintage Shops

Porto’s vintage scene has exploded in recent years, with a fantastic array of shops catering to every style and budget. Tucked away on winding streets, these boutiques are treasure chests of fashion history, each with its own carefully selected collection.

For Every Style and Budget

Starting with a personal favourite, Mão Esquerda Vintage (Rua da Alegria 5) is a haven for true vintage lovers, offering beautifully preserved pieces for men and women. It is especially strong on Asian vintage from the 1940s to the 1980s. For a completely different vibe, look up Chiclete Shop, the go-to spot for 90s and 2000s streetwear, denim and cowboy shirts. Listings give more than one address, so check its Instagram for the current shop before you go. Nearby, Mon Père Vintage (Largo de Alberto Pimentel 38) has been a city staple since 2013. I love how colourful and well-organized their selection is, making it easy to find everything from cowboy boots to funky patterned shirts.

The Thrill of the Bargain (and a Coffee Break)

If you love the thrill of the hunt, you cannot miss O'Kilo on the famous Rua de Santa Catarina (number 601). Here, you pay by weight, with clothing at €10 per kilo (the 2024 price). It takes some digging, but the potential for finding an incredible bargain is immense. When you’ve worked up an appetite from all that searching, make your way to Com Sotaque (Rua do Alferes Malheiro 118-120). It's another good stop for second-hand clothes in the same part of town.

Curated and High-End Finds

For a more curated experience, check out Wild at Heart (Rua do Almada 473). It's a small but mighty shop with a beautifully selected range of seasonal vintage pieces, from 80s sportswear to timeless trench coats. And for those seeking true luxury, Yeahllow Store (Edifício do Infante, Avenida da Boavista 3769, shop 10) specializes in authenticated high-end vintage from the 1950s to the 1990s. Check its opening times before you make the trip out to Boavista.

Made in Porto: Discovering Artisan Crafts & Local Design

Beyond vintage, Porto is a city brimming with contemporary creativity. Supporting local artists and designers is one of the best ways to connect with the city’s modern identity and find a souvenir that tells a real story.

The Creative Epicentre: Rua de Miguel Bombarda

Often called the "Art Block," Rua de Miguel Bombarda is the beating heart of Porto’s contemporary art scene. The street is lined with independent art galleries and unique boutiques. Don't miss the Centro Comercial Bombarda (at number 285), an alternative shopping centre that houses a collection of creative stores selling everything from local fashion to handmade goods. It’s a one-stop-shop for Porto's creative output.

Portuguese Classics and Creative Hubs

For a beautifully curated selection of Portuguese heritage products, A Vida Portuguesa is unparalleled. Its first Porto shop on Rua de Cândido dos Reis closed in 2020, and today you'll find it inside Time Out Market at São Bento station. It celebrates traditional craftsmanship, offering everything from classic soaps and stationery to gorgeous ceramics and textiles. Another fantastic, multi-purpose spot is Embaixada do Porto (Praça de Carlos Alberto 121), which is part analogue photography shop, part record store, and part bar that hosts exhibitions and live music. If you want to buy directly from emerging designers, head to CRU Cowork. This dynamic space is a coworking hub, gallery, and concept store all in one.

Get Your Hands Dirty: Artisan Workshops

Why not create your own souvenir? Porto has numerous workshops where you can connect with traditional crafts. Domus Arte runs two-hour beginner workshops in Sé (Rua da Bainharia) and Lapa (Rua de Antero de Quental) where you paint your own azulejo (the Portuguese tile) and take it home. Its Lapa shop also does ceramic painting. It's an unforgettable way to engage with local culture and take home a piece of Porto that is uniquely yours.

Happy hunting! My final tip: always carry a bit of cash with you. While card payments are common, many market stalls and some small independent shops still prefer cash. It’s a small detail that will ensure your day of discovery goes off without a hitch.
