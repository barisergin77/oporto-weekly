Hello fellow Porto lovers,

It’s Baris here, from Oporto Weekly. One of the questions I get asked most often by visitors and newcomers alike is, "Where can I find something truly special to take home?" While the city is famous for its Port wine and beautifully tiled façades, there’s a whole other world of shopping here that goes far beyond the typical souvenir shop. I’m talking about a vibrant, breathing ecosystem of markets, vintage boutiques, and artisan workshops where the city's creative soul truly shines.

Forget the mass-produced trinkets. Today, I want to take you on a personal tour of my favourite spots to unearth unique treasures. This is your definitive guide for 2025 and 2026 to shopping like a local in Porto, whether you’re hunting for a one-of-a-kind vintage jacket, a piece of art from an emerging designer, or simply the thrill of a flea market bargain. Let’s dive in.

The Soul of the City: Porto's Vibrant Markets

There’s no better way to feel the pulse of Porto than by wandering through one of its bustling markets. The air buzzes with chatter, the scent of fresh flowers and grilled sardines hangs in the air, and every stall holds a potential discovery. Each market has its own distinct personality, so you can easily find one that suits your style.

The Iconic: Mercado do Bolhão

If you only have time for one market, make it the Mercado do Bolhão. After a magnificent restoration, this two-story iron landmark is more beautiful than ever. While the ground floor is a feast for the senses with vendors selling fresh produce, glistening fish, and local cheeses, don’t miss the stalls offering lovely handicrafts. When you need a break, head to the upper level for the fantastic food court, home to about ten restaurants perfect for a quick and delicious lunch.

  • Address: Rua Formosa 322, 4000-248 Porto
  • 2026 Hours: Monday to Friday, 8:00 AM – 8:00 PM; Saturday, 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM. Closed Sundays.

The Treasure Hunt: Feira da Vandoma

For the true bargain hunters and lovers of the delightfully unexpected, the Feira da Vandoma is an absolute must. Every Saturday morning, this sprawling flea market transforms Avenida 25 de Abril into a paradise of pre-loved goods. You can find everything here: vinyl records, quirky furniture, antique tools, piles of clothes, and boxes of old books. My advice? Get there early, as the best items are snapped up quickly, and many vendors start packing up around 1:00 PM. And remember, bargaining is part of the fun, so have your cash ready and don’t be shy!

  • Address: Avenida 25 de Abril, Porto
  • 2026 Hours: Every Saturday, 8:00 AM – 1:00 PM.

The Curated Cool: Mercado Porto Belo

Inspired by London’s famous Portobello Market, this Saturday gathering at Praça de Carlos Alberto offers a more curated and artisanal experience. It’s the perfect place to find high-quality vintage clothing, handmade jewellery from local designers, organic products, and a fantastic selection of vinyl records. The vibe is relaxed and creative, making it a wonderful spot to spend a Saturday afternoon, browsing the stalls and soaking in the atmosphere.

  • Address: Praça de Carlos Alberto, Porto
  • 2026 Hours: Every Saturday, 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM.

The Modern Pop-Up: Urban Market

If your taste leans more contemporary, keep an eye out for the Urban Market. This pop-up event showcases the work of Porto’s independent designers and artists, offering everything from cutting-edge fashion to unique home decor. Its location is fluid—you might find it at Praça das Cardosas on a sunny day or inside the iconic Hard Club when it’s raining. Your best bet is to check their social media channels for the latest dates and venues before you head out.

A Trip Back in Time: Porto’s Best Vintage Shops

Porto’s vintage scene has exploded in recent years, with a fantastic array of shops catering to every style and budget. Tucked away on winding streets, these boutiques are treasure chests of fashion history, each with its own carefully selected collection.

For Every Style and Budget

Starting with a personal favourite, Mão Esquerda Vintage (Rua da Alegria 5) is a haven for true vintage lovers, offering beautifully preserved pieces for men and women. They have a particularly stunning collection of vintage Japanese kimonos that are simply works of art. For a completely different vibe, head to ChicleteShop. As of 2025, they’ve moved to a fantastic new spot at Praça dos Poveiros (Rua Santo Ildefonso, 187) and are celebrating their 10th anniversary! It’s the go-to spot for vintage streetwear – think classic sports jerseys, Carhartt pants, and perfectly worn-in Hawaiian shirts. Nearby, Mon Père Vintage (Largo de Alberto Pimentel 38) has been a city staple since 2013. I love how colourful and well-organized their selection is, making it easy to find everything from cowboy boots to funky patterned shirts. Just a heads-up: this charming spot is cash only.

The Thrill of the Bargain (and a Coffee Break)

If you love the thrill of the hunt, you cannot miss O'Kilo on the famous Rua de Santa Catarina (number 601). Here, you pay by weight, with clothing priced at a jaw-dropping €10 per kilo. It takes some digging, but the potential for finding an incredible bargain is immense. When you’ve worked up an appetite from all that searching, make your way to Com Sotaque (Rua do Alferes Malheiro 118-120). This brilliant concept combines a vintage store with a cozy café, allowing you to sip a coffee while surrounded by unique finds. It’s the perfect mid-shop pit stop.

Curated and High-End Finds

For a more curated experience, check out Wild at Heart (Rua do Almada 473). It's a small but mighty shop with a beautifully selected range of seasonal vintage pieces, from 80s sportswear to timeless trench coats. And for those seeking true luxury, Yeahllow Store (Avenida da Boavista 3769) specializes in authenticated high-end vintage from the 1950s to the 1990s. To ensure a personalized and intimate shopping experience, visits are by appointment only, so be sure to book ahead.

A quick note for fellow treasure hunters: You may see older guides mention a large warehouse called Armazém. Please be aware that this venue permanently closed at the end of 2022, so you can save yourself a trip.

Made in Porto: Discovering Artisan Crafts & Local Design

Beyond vintage, Porto is a city brimming with contemporary creativity. Supporting local artists and designers is one of the best ways to connect with the city’s modern identity and find a souvenir that tells a real story.

The Creative Epicentre: Rua de Miguel Bombarda

Often called the "Art Block," Rua de Miguel Bombarda is the beating heart of Porto’s contemporary art scene. The street is lined with independent art galleries and unique boutiques. Don't miss the Centro Comercial Bombarda (at number 285), an alternative shopping centre that houses a collection of creative stores selling everything from local fashion to handmade goods. It’s a one-stop-shop for Porto's creative output.

Portuguese Classics and Creative Hubs

For a beautifully curated selection of Portuguese heritage products, A Vida Portuguesa is unparalleled. Located in a stunning corner of the São Bento Railway Station, this shop celebrates traditional craftsmanship, offering everything from classic soaps and stationery to gorgeous ceramics and textiles. Another fantastic, multi-purpose spot is Embaixada do Porto (Praça de Carlos Alberto 121), which is part analogue photography shop, part record store, and part bar that hosts exhibitions and live music. If you want to buy directly from emerging designers, head to CRU Cowork. This dynamic space is a coworking hub, gallery, and concept store all in one. I recently spotted a locally designed 2026 wall planner for €16.80 and a beautiful hoodie for €99.00 – proof of the incredible talent brewing here.

Get Your Hands Dirty: Artisan Workshops

Why not create your own souvenir? Porto has numerous workshops where you can connect with traditional crafts. Studios like Domus Arte and Matérica offer fantastic workshops for beginners where you can learn to paint your own azulejos (the iconic Portuguese tiles) or try your hand at ceramics. Domus Arte even offers workshops in jewelry making, including the intricate art of Portuguese filigree. It's an unforgettable way to engage with local culture and take home a piece of Porto that is uniquely yours.

Happy hunting! My final tip: always carry a bit of cash with you. While card payments are common, many market stalls and smaller independent shops, like Mon Père, still operate cash-only. It’s a small detail that will ensure your day of discovery goes off without a hitch.
