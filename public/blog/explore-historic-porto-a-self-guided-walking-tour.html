The historic centre of Porto is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and it's small enough to cover on foot. Here are three walks I'd give a friend: the classic route from Aliados down to the river, a shorter one for azulejo tiles, and a quieter downhill walk through the old town. Wear shoes with grip. The hills are steep and the granite gets slippery when it rains.

The Classic Walk: Aliados to the Ribeira and Across to Gaia

This is the one to do if you only have a day. It's mostly downhill once you've climbed to the Sé, and with stops and queues it will fill most of the day.

Avenida dos Aliados and Praça da Liberdade

Start in the city's main square. At the top of the avenue is the Câmara Municipal (City Hall) with its clock tower, and at the bottom is the statue of King Pedro IV on horseback.

  • Where: Praça da Liberdade, 4000-322 Porto

São Bento Station

A few steps away, walk into the main hall of São Bento. It's lined with about 20,000 blue and white azulejo tiles by Jorge Colaço, showing battles, royal events and the history of transport in Portugal. It's a working station, so it's free to go in.

  • Where: Praça de Almeida Garrett, 4000-069 Porto

Porto Cathedral (Sé do Porto)

From São Bento it's uphill to the Sé, the Romanesque cathedral that looks more like a fortress. The church is free. The Gothic cloister, with its 18th-century tile panels, and the terrace with views over the Ribeira rooftops are a paid visit.

  • Where: Terreiro da Sé, 4050-573 Porto
  • When: Daily, 9:00 to 18:30 from April to October and 9:00 to 17:30 from November to March; closed at Christmas and Easter. See diocese-porto.pt

Clérigos Church and Tower

The baroque bell tower is about 75 metres tall and you can see it from almost anywhere in the city. The climb is narrow and steep, and the view from the top covers the whole centre, the river and, on a clear day, the sea.

  • Where: Rua de São Filipe de Nery, 4050-546 Porto
  • When: Daily, 9:00 to 19:00 (last entry 30 minutes before closing). Night visits to the tower run only at Easter, in summer and at Christmas.
  • Price: €10 for the tower and museum, €7 students, free for children up to 10; night tower ticket €5. Book at torredosclerigos.pt

Livraria Lello

Yes, it's famous and it's busy, but the carved wood and the curving red staircase are worth seeing once. You need a timed ticket, which also works as a voucher towards a book from Lello's own editions. Book online to skip most of the queue.

  • Where: Rua das Carmelitas 144, 4050-161 Porto
  • When: Daily, 9:00 to 19:30
  • Tickets: tickets.livrarialello.pt

Rua das Flores

Walk down Rua das Flores towards the river. It's pedestrian, lined with shops, cafés and old houses with iron balconies, and a good place to stop for a coffee.

Palácio da Bolsa

The 19th-century stock exchange building can only be seen on a guided tour. The rooms get grander as you go, and the tour ends in the Arab Room, covered in gilded Moorish-revival stucco. You choose the tour time and language at the ticket office.

  • Where: Rua de Ferreira Borges, 4050-253 Porto
  • When: Open 9:00 to 18:30; it closes to visitors on some dates for events, so check palaciodabolsa.com
  • Price: €14 for the guided tour

Church of São Francisco

Right next to the Bolsa. The Gothic outside is plain. The inside is covered in gilded carved wood, almost every surface of it. The ticket also covers the catacombs and a small museum.

  • Where: Rua do Infante D. Henrique, 4050-297 Porto
  • When: Ticket office open daily, 9:00 to 20:00 from April to September and 9:00 to 19:00 from October to March; closed 25 December. Check the price at ordemsaofranciscoporto.pt

Cais da Ribeira and the Dom Luís I Bridge

Down at the river, the Ribeira is the postcard view: tall, narrow, colourful houses and rabelo boats moored along the quay. Then cross the Dom Luís I Bridge. The upper deck has the better view, but it's a long climb back up from the Ribeira; the lower deck starts right at the quay.

  • Where: Cais da Ribeira, 4050-512 Porto

Finish in Vila Nova de Gaia

On the Gaia side you're among the port wine lodges, with names like Sandeman, Taylor's and Graham's. Most offer a tour with a tasting; prices vary a lot by house and by the wines you taste, so compare on their own websites.

The Azulejo Walk

A shorter walk for tiles. All of these are on the outside of buildings or in a station, so they're free and you can do it at any time of day.

São Bento Station

Start with the Jorge Colaço panels again, and this time take your time. Besides the big historical scenes, look up: the coloured frieze along the top tells the history of transport.

  • Where: Praça de Almeida Garrett, 4000-069 Porto

Capela das Almas

Walk up Rua de Santa Catarina, the main shopping street, to this chapel. Its outside walls are covered in almost 16,000 tiles showing scenes from the lives of saints.

  • Where: Rua de Santa Catarina 428, 4000-124 Porto

Igreja de Santo Ildefonso

Back down towards Praça da Batalha, the façade of this 18th-century church has tile panels by Jorge Colaço as well, so it pairs well with São Bento.

  • Where: Praça da Batalha, Porto

Carmo and Carmelitas Churches

Two churches that look like one, separated by a house about a metre wide. The side wall of the Igreja do Carmo has one of the largest tile murals in the city.

  • Where: Rua do Carmo, 4050-164 Porto

The Quiet Way Down: Viewpoints and Old Streets

A more relaxed walk, mostly downhill, away from the main crowds. It ends near the river.

Centro Português de Fotografia

Start at the old Cadeia da Relação, a former prison that now houses the national photography centre. Entry is free, and walking through the old cells is part of the visit.

  • Where: Largo Amor de Perdição, 4050-008 Porto
  • When: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; see cpf.pt

Passeio das Virtudes

A short walk away, this terraced garden faces west over the Douro and the Arrábida Bridge. Locals come here for the sunset.

Miradouro da Vitória

A viewpoint on private land that is left open to the public, on Rua de São Bento da Vitória. From here you see the Dom Luís I Bridge, the Sé, the Ribeira rooftops and the port lodges in Gaia in one frame.

Rua da Bainharia

Keep heading down and you reach one of the oldest and narrowest streets in the city. It's dark and quiet, and it gives you a good idea of what the medieval town felt like.

Tips for Walking in Porto

  • Shoes: Comfortable, with a good sole. The hills are real and the cobbles are uneven.
  • Book the big ones: Livraria Lello and the Clérigos Tower sell timed tickets online. Booking a day ahead saves a lot of queuing in high season.
  • Carry a little cash: Most places take cards, but a few euros helps for a coffee or a pastel de nata on the way.
  • Leave the map sometimes: If a side street looks interesting, take it. The centre is small and you won't get lost for long.
