Baris here. The question I get most from friends who are planning a visit is "Where should we stay?" Porto is small, but each part of it feels different, and where you sleep changes what your days look like. Below are the neighbourhoods I send people to, with a few places to stay, eat and see in each.

Ribeira: The Riverside Old Town

Ribeira is the Porto of the postcards: tiled houses stacked down the hill to the Douro, inside the UNESCO-listed historic centre. It is the busiest part of the city with visitors, day and night. If it is your first time, you will want to spend at least an evening here anyway.

Where to Stay in Ribeira

  • Pestana Vintage Porto Hotel & World Heritage Site: A 5-star hotel in a row of old buildings right on the main square, with river-view rooms. Praça da Ribeira 1, 4050-513 Porto.
  • 1872 River House: A small guesthouse with river views, a quieter alternative to the square.
  • Porto A.S. 1829 Hotel: A boutique hotel in the old Araújo & Sobrinho stationery shop, between Rua das Flores and Largo de São Domingos. Largo de São Domingos 45, 4050-545 Porto.

Where to Eat in Ribeira

  • Adega São Nicolau: A long-running tavern doing traditional Portuguese food, with fish as the main event. Open 12:00 to 22:30, closed on Sundays. Rua de São Nicolau 1, 4050-561 Porto.
  • Taberna dos Mercadores: Run by the same family as Adega São Nicolau, a few doors away. There are only eight tables, so book. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 12:30 to 22:30. Rua dos Mercadores 36-38, 4050-373 Porto.
  • Wine Quay Bar: For a glass of Portuguese wine and a board of cheese and charcuterie on the riverfront, looking at the Dom Luís I Bridge. Muro dos Bacalhoeiros 111-112, 4050-080 Porto.

What to Explore in Ribeira

  • Dom Luís I Bridge: Walk across the upper deck. You get Ribeira on one side and the Port lodges of Gaia on the other, and it costs nothing.
  • Palácio da Bolsa: The 19th-century stock exchange building, seen on a guided visit of about 30 minutes that ends in the Arab Room. The guided visit costs €14; book on the official site. Rua de Ferreira Borges, 4050-253 Porto.
  • Igreja de São Francisco: Gothic outside, and inside one of the richest displays of gilded carved wood (talha dourada) in Portugal. The visit includes the catacombs and a small museum. Check times on the Ordem de São Francisco site. Rua do Infante D. Henrique, 4050-297 Porto.
  • Six bridges cruise: The classic 50-minute trip on a rabelo-style boat, leaving from Ribeira or the Gaia side. Prices start at around €18 with the operators I checked.

Baixa: Downtown

Baixa is the area up the hill from the river around Avenida dos Aliados, São Bento station and Rua de Santa Catarina. It is the most practical base: flat-ish streets by Porto standards, the main metro stations, and most sights within walking distance.

Where to Stay in Baixa

  • InterContinental Porto - Palácio das Cardosas: A restored 18th-century palace taking up a whole block at the bottom of Avenida dos Aliados. Praça da Liberdade 25, 4000-322 Porto.

Where to Eat in Baixa

  • Casa Guedes: The roast pork sandwich (sandes de pernil) place, around since 1987. Order it with queijo da serra. There are now several branches; the original is on Praça dos Poveiros. Praça dos Poveiros 130, Porto.
  • Cantina 32: Small plates to share on Rua das Flores. Rua das Flores 32, 4050-262 Porto.
  • Tapabento: Next to São Bento station, with a menu built on market produce. It is small and closed on Mondays and Tuesdays, so book. Rua da Madeira 221, 4000-330 Porto.
  • MUU Steakhouse: A steakhouse focused on high-quality cuts of beef. Rua do Almada 149, 4050-037 Porto.

What to Explore in Baixa

  • São Bento station: Step inside for the azulejo panels in the entrance hall, around 20,000 tiles telling Portuguese history. Praça de Almeida Garrett.
  • Torre dos Clérigos: Climb the bell tower for the view over the rooftops. The tower and museum ticket costs €10 (students €7, under-10s free), open daily 9:00 to 19:00, with later hours at Easter, in summer and at Christmas. Details on the official site. Rua de São Filipe de Nery, 4050-546 Porto.
  • Livraria Lello: The neo-Gothic bookshop with the red staircase. It is open daily from 9:00 to 19:30, entry is by ticket, and the ticket works as a voucher towards a book. Buy online on the Lello site to skip part of the queue. Rua das Carmelitas 144, 4050-161 Porto.
  • Mercado do Bolhão: The restored city market. The fresh market is open Monday to Friday 8:00 to 19:00 and Saturday 8:00 to 18:00; closed on Sundays and holidays. Rua Formosa / Rua Fernandes Tomás.

Cedofeita: The Arts District

A short walk uphill from the centre, Cedofeita is where you find the contemporary art galleries of Rua de Miguel Bombarda, independent shops and brunch places. It is more residential and quieter at night than Baixa.

Where to Stay in Cedofeita

  • Rosa Et Al Townhouse: A seven-suite townhouse with a garden where they serve brunch every day from 9:00 to 17:00. Rua do Rosário 233, 4050-524 Porto.
  • Gallery Hostel: A hostel in restored 19th-century buildings, with private rooms and suites as well as dorms. Rua de Miguel Bombarda 222, 4050-377 Porto.
  • Casa Carolina: A small guesthouse on the neighbourhood's main street. When I checked, its Tripadvisor listing said it was temporarily closed until the end of November 2026, so check it has reopened. Rua de Cedofeita 159, 4050-179 Porto.

Where to Eat in Cedofeita

  • Zenith Brunch & Cocktails: All-day brunch: pancakes, eggs benedict, bowls. Expect a queue at weekends. Praça de Carlos Alberto 86, 4050-158 Porto.

What to Explore in Cedofeita

  • Rua de Miguel Bombarda: Spend an afternoon walking in and out of the galleries. The Centro Comercial Bombarda, a small mall of independent shops, is on the same street.
  • Museu Nacional Soares dos Reis: Portuguese painting and sculpture in the Carrancas palace. Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00; general ticket €10. See the museum site. Rua de Dom Manuel II 56.
  • Jardins do Palácio de Cristal: 19th-century gardens with peacocks and views down to the Douro, right next to the museum.

Foz do Douro: By the Sea

Foz is where the river meets the Atlantic: a seafront promenade, small beaches and some of the city's best restaurants. It is calmer and more expensive than the centre.

Where to Stay in Foz do Douro

  • Vila Foz Hotel & Spa: A 5-star hotel in a restored 19th-century mansion facing the sea. Its Vila Foz Restaurant, led by chef Arnaldo Azevedo, has a Michelin star. Avenida de Montevideu 236, 4150-516 Porto.
  • Hotel Boa-Vista: A 3-star hotel on the seafront with a rooftop pool. Esplanada do Castelo 58, 4150-196 Porto.
  • HF Ipanema Park: A 5-star hotel between Foz and the centre, with the Ontop rooftop bar and pool. Rua de Serralves 124, 4150-702 Porto.

Where to Eat in Foz do Douro

  • Pedro Lemos: Chef Pedro Lemos's restaurant in an old stone house, with one Michelin star and a seasonal tasting menu. Rua do Padre Luís Cabral 974, 4150-459 Porto.
  • Cafeína: A Foz fixture for more than 25 years, with French- and Portuguese-influenced cooking. Rua do Padrão 100, 4150-557 Porto.
  • Casa de Pasto da Palmeira: Petiscos to share, with a view of the river mouth. Open Monday to Saturday 12:00 to 24:00, Sunday 12:00 to 19:00. Rua do Passeio Alegre, Foz do Douro.
  • Praia da Luz: A restaurant, café and terrace on the beach, open since 1988. Good for a long lunch or a drink at sunset.

What to Explore in Foz do Douro

  • Farol de Felgueiras: Walk out along the breakwater to the small lighthouse at the river mouth. On rough days the waves come right over the pier, so keep your distance.
  • Serralves: The contemporary art museum designed by Álvaro Siza, the Art Deco Casa de Serralves and a large park. A ticket for all spaces costs €24; check exhibitions and times on the Serralves site. Rua D. João de Castro 210, 4150-417 Porto.
  • Jardim do Passeio Alegre: A 19th-century riverside garden with tall palm trees, good for a walk before dinner.
  • SEA LIFE Porto: The aquarium near Castelo do Queijo, an easy pick with children. 1ª Rua Particular do Castelo do Queijo, 4100-429 Porto.

Bonfim: East of the Centre

Bonfim, just east of the centre, is mostly residential, with old shops, traditional restaurants and a growing number of cafés. There are far fewer visitors here, which is the point.

Where to Stay in Bonfim

  • Pestana Palácio do Freixo: An 18th-century palace on the riverbank, classified as a national monument and now a pousada. Estrada Nacional 108, 4300-316 Porto.
  • Vila Galé Porto Centro: A 4-star hotel by the Campo 24 de Agosto metro station. Avenida de Fernão de Magalhães 7, 4300-190 Porto.
  • The Editory Artist Baixa Porto Hotel: A 5-star boutique hotel in a building that was once a factory and then an art school, on the edge of Bonfim and Baixa. Rua da Firmeza 49, 4000-228 Porto.

Where to Eat in Bonfim

  • A Cozinha do Manel: Northern Portuguese home cooking in a simple dining room. Rua do Heroísmo 215, 4300-259 Porto.
  • Euskalduna Studio: Chef Vasco Coelho Santos's counter restaurant, with one Michelin star and a tasting menu. Book well ahead. Rua de Santo Ildefonso 404, 4000-466 Porto.
  • Casa Nanda: A family restaurant open since 1978, doing traditional food from a wood-fired oven. Always full of locals. Rua da Alegria 394, 4000-035 Porto.

What to Explore in Bonfim

  • Miradouro das Fontainhas: A sunset viewpoint looking over the Douro and the Dom Luís I Bridge, with fewer people than the viewpoints in the centre.
  • Cemitério do Prado do Repouso: Porto's first public cemetery, full of 19th-century funerary sculpture. A quiet walk.
  • Igreja do Bonfim: The parish church that gives the neighbourhood its name. Rua do Bonfim, 4300-070 Porto.
  • Museu Militar do Porto: The army's military history museum, on the same street as A Cozinha do Manel. Rua do Heroísmo 329, 4300-259 Porto.

Vila Nova de Gaia: Across the River

Gaia is technically its own city, but its riverfront faces Ribeira and it is where the Port wine lodges are. The views back at Porto from this side are the best you will get.

Where to Stay in Gaia

  • The Yeatman: A wine-themed luxury hotel on the hill above the lodges. Its gastronomic restaurant has two Michelin stars. Rua do Choupelo, 4400-088 Vila Nova de Gaia.

Where to Eat in Gaia

  • Enoteca 17.56: The restaurant at Real Companhia Velha, with a terrace facing Porto and a long Douro wine list. Rua de Serpa Pinto 44B, 4400-307 Vila Nova de Gaia.

What to Explore in Gaia

  • Port wine lodges: Most of the big names, including Taylor's and Graham's, run tours and tastings. Book ahead in summer.
  • Jardim do Morro and the cable car: The Teleférico de Gaia runs a five-minute ride between the riverside (by the Cais de Gaia market) and Jardim do Morro, next to the upper deck of the bridge. The garden is where people sit on the grass at sunset. Times and prices are on the cable car site.
  • WOW (World of Wine): A cultural quarter in old lodges with several museums on wine, cork and the region, plus restaurants and a terrace. One day pass covers the museums; see wow.pt.

Pick one of these as your base and walk to the others. Porto is built on hills, so bring shoes you can walk in all day.
