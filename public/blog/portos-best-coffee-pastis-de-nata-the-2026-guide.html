Hello, Porto lovers, Baris here. Coffee in Porto comes in two kinds. There's the neighbourhood pastelaria, where you drink a quick espresso at the counter with a pastry, and there's the newer specialty scene, with single-origin beans and filter coffee. This guide covers both, plus the places I go for a pastel de nata.

Pastéis de Nata

The custard tart is from Lisbon, but you can eat a very good one in Porto. Look for a crisp, flaky crust and a custard with dark, blistered spots on top, and eat it warm.

Manteigaria

Manteigaria is the name most people give you first. They bake in the shop, so you can watch the pastry being folded and the tarts coming out of the oven, and they're served warm. Porto has four of them.

  • Where: by Bolhão market (Rua de Alexandre Braga 24), on Rua de Santa Catarina, at Clérigos and on Largo de São Domingos; addresses on manteigaria.com

Castro: Atelier de Pastéis de Nata

Castro makes only natas, with a very buttery, delicate pastry. It's on Mouzinho da Silveira, the street that runs down from São Bento to Ribeira, so it's easy to fit in on the way to the river.

  • Where: Rua de Mouzinho da Silveira 61, 4050-420 Porto
  • When: every day, 8:00 to 22:00 (per its Google Maps listing)

Fábrica da Nata

Fábrica da Nata serves its tarts warm and has room to sit down with a coffee, which helps on busy Santa Catarina.

  • Where: Rua de Santa Catarina 331-335; Praça de Almeida Garrett 7 (opposite São Bento station)

Two More Nata Stops

For a neighbourhood bakery, Padaria Ribeiro on Praça de Guilherme Gomes Fernandes 21 is open every day from 8:00 to 20:00. Natas d'Ouro on Rua de Sá da Bandeira 148 makes several flavoured versions alongside the classic one.

Casa Berlim: The Bola de Berlim Detour

Porto's other great pastry is the bola de Berlim, a doughnut, and Casa Berlim is built around it. The small shop on Rua da Ponte Nova, a few minutes' walk from São Bento, fries batches through the day and serves them warm, com creme (custard-filled) or sem creme (plain), with specialty coffee. It closes for an hour at lunchtime.

  • Where: Rua da Ponte Nova 58, 4050-485 Porto

Specialty Coffee

I still drink plenty of plain espressos at the counter, but the specialty cafés are where you go for a pour-over, a flat white or a bag of freshly roasted beans to take home.

7G Roaster

Walk across the Dom Luís I Bridge into Vila Nova de Gaia and you'll find 7G Roaster, which roasts its own beans and runs a café with a brunch menu and outdoor seating. It's close to the Port lodges, so it's a good first stop before a cellar visit.

  • Where: Rua de França 10, Vila Nova de Gaia

Combi Coffee Roasters

Combi was one of the first specialty roasters in Porto. The café is small and simple, they roast their own beans, and it's where I'd go for a well-made black coffee or a flat white.

  • Where: Rua do Morgado de Mateus 29, 4000-334 Porto
  • When: every day, 9:00 to 17:00 (per its Google Maps listing)

SO Coffee Roasters and C'alma

For more serious coffee, SO Coffee Roasters roasts its own and has two cafés. C'alma Coffee Room rotates beans from other European roasters and brews them several ways. C'alma has moved from its old Passos Manuel address and is now upstairs on Rua de Sampaio Bruno.

  • SO: Rua de Sá de Noronha 119; Rua da Restauração 455
  • C'alma: Rua de Sampaio Bruno 12, 2nd floor, 4000-439 Porto; Monday to Saturday, 9:00 to 17:00

Época

Época is a small, bright café known for weekday brunch: sourdough with butters and dips, a mostly vegetarian menu and plant milks. It keeps short hours and is closed at weekends.

  • Where: Rua do Rosário 22, 4050-522 Porto
  • When: Monday to Friday, 10:00 to 15:00

Majestic Café

The Majestic Café on Rua de Santa Catarina opened in December 1921, under the name Elite, and has one of the city's finest Art Nouveau interiors: carved wood, mirrors and chandeliers. It's full of visitors and costs more than the neighbourhood pastelaria, but it's worth sitting down here once.

  • Where: Rua de Santa Catarina 112, 4000-442 Porto
  • When: Monday to Saturday, 9:00 to 23:00; closed Sunday

Ordering Coffee in Porto

A neighbourhood café is still one of the cheapest places to sit down in the city. Specialty cafés charge more, as you'd expect.

  • Um café / um cimbalino: an espresso. Cimbalino is the Porto word; in Lisbon they say bica.
  • Um pingo: an espresso with a drop (pingo) of milk.
  • Uma meia de leite: half coffee, half hot milk, in a cup.
  • Um galão: espresso topped up with hot milk in a tall glass.

My last tip: most counters have shakers of cinnamon and icing sugar next to the natas. Cinnamon is the traditional choice, and the one I'd pick.
