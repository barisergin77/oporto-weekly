Hello Porto lovers, Baris here. One of the questions I get most often from visitors and new residents alike is, "Where do you shop?" And they don't mean the big international chains on Rua de Santa Catarina. They're asking about the places with soul, the spots where you can find a piece of Porto's history, a flash of its creative side, or something you simply won't see anywhere else. For me, a lot of this city's character lives in its independent shops and busy markets.

Forget fast fashion and mass-produced souvenirs. Today, I'm taking you on a personal tour of my favourite spots for vintage, artisan and one-off finds. So, grab a comfortable pair of shoes, and let's go exploring.

The Soul of the City: Porto's Markets

There's no better way to tap into the city's rhythm than by spending a Saturday morning wandering through one of its open-air markets. The chatter of vendors, the smell of street food, the hunt for a bargain. Each market has its own personality.

For the Trendy & Eclectic: Mercado Porto Belo

If you're looking for a market with a relaxed, creative feel, this is your place. Inspired by London's Portobello Road, Mercado Porto Belo is where Porto's hip crowd gathers. It's my go-to for a relaxed Saturday stroll. You'll find a well-chosen selection of goods from local artists and collectors. I've lost count of the number of vinyl records and vintage band t-shirts I've picked up here over the years. It's also a good spot for handmade jewellery, antique cameras and prints. There's often live music in the square.

  • Where: Praça de Carlos Alberto, 4050-157 Porto
  • When: Saturdays; opening hours change with the season, so check Agenda Porto before you go
  • My Tip: Don't be in a rush. Grab a coffee from a nearby café, find a spot to sit, and just take in the scene.

The Classic Treasure Hunt: Feira da Vandoma

Now, for something completely different. Feira da Vandoma is Porto's oldest and best-known flea market, and it is a sprawling chaos of everything imaginable. Nothing here is curated; it's a proper, old-school treasure hunt. You'll see tables piled high with everything from rusty tools and old family photos to vintage furniture, ceramics and mountains of second-hand clothes. You have to be willing to dig, but the rewards are well worth it.

  • Where: Avenida 25 de Abril, Campanhã, 4300-537 Porto
  • When: Every Saturday, 8:00 AM to 1:00 PM
  • My Tip: The early bird truly gets the worm here. Arrive as close to 8:00 AM as you can for the best selection, bring cash, and don't be afraid to haggle. It's all part of the fun.

A Riverside Delight: Mercado da Alegria

Set in the Jardim do Passeio Alegre, where the Douro River meets the Atlantic, this market is a quieter affair. The Mercado da Alegria is a mix of artisan crafts, local produce and vintage clothing. It's one of my favourite places to pick up edible souvenirs, like locally made sausages and cheeses, as well as gifts made by local artisans. The seaside setting makes it a good Sunday outing.

  • Where: Jardim do Passeio Alegre, Rua do Passeio Alegre, 4150-571 Porto
  • When: Sundays from 9:00 AM; dates vary, so check Agenda Porto before you go

The Food Market: Mercado do Bolhão

The Mercado do Bolhão reopened after a long restoration, and the two-storey iron market is busier than ever. The ground floor is fruit and veg, fish and cheese, with a few stalls selling crafts. Upstairs there are food stalls and restaurants if you want to stop for lunch.

  • Where: Rua Formosa, Baixa (side entrances on Rua de Sá da Bandeira and Rua Alexandre Braga)
  • When: Monday to Friday 8:00 to 20:00, Saturday 8:00 to 18:00. Closed Sundays and public holidays. Details on mercadobolhao.pt

The Pop-Up: Urban Market

The Urban Market is a pop-up for Porto's independent designers and makers, with clothes, homeware and prints. It moves around: past editions have been at Praça das Cardosas, Praça da Alegria and indoors at the Hard Club. Check its social media for the next date and venue.

Celebrating Portuguese Craft: Artisan & Design Shops

Beyond the markets, Porto has a growing number of shops dedicated to both traditional Portuguese craft and contemporary design. These are the places to go for a meaningful souvenir, a good-quality gift, or an object with a story behind it.

A Nostalgic Journey: A Vida Portuguesa

Walking into A Vida Portuguesa is like stepping back in time, in the best possible way. The shop is dedicated to classic Portuguese brands, many of which have been around for generations. The shelves are filled with beautifully packaged goods, from stationery and perfume to food, textiles, crafts and toys. It's my first stop for Portuguese gifts that feel both timeless and special. The old shop on Rua de Cândido dos Reis closed in 2020; the brand came back to Porto in 2024 with a shop inside Time Out Market.

  • Where: Time Out Market Porto, south wing of São Bento Station, Praça de Almeida Garrett

The Creative Hub: CRU Cowork

CRU Cowork is part coworking space, part art gallery and part concept store, and it's a place where you can buy directly from the city's emerging designers. The selection is always changing, with everything from fashion and jewellery to bold illustrations and homewares. If you want to support local talent and find something contemporary, this is where you need to be.

  • Where: Rua do Rosário 211, 4050-524 Porto

A Legacy of Luxury: Claus Porto

With a history stretching back to 1887, Claus Porto is a legend in the world of soaps and fragrances. Their flagship store on Rua das Flores is a beautiful, museum-like space. The products are known for their rich scents and Art Deco-inspired packaging, and a bar of their soap makes an elegant souvenir. Upstairs, a floor tells the story of the brand.

  • Where: Rua das Flores 22, 4050-262 Porto
  • When: Every day, 10:00 AM to 7:00 PM

Records, Film and a Bar: Embaixada do Porto

Embaixada do Porto is part analogue photography shop, part record shop and part bar, and it hosts exhibitions and live music. It's on the same square as Mercado Porto Belo, so pair the two on a Saturday.

  • Where: Praça de Carlos Alberto 121, Porto

Portuguese Design: Mercado 48

Mercado 48 is a small concept store in the Baixa selling Portuguese-made design and some vintage: homeware, bags, prints and T-shirts.

  • Where: Rua da Conceição 48, 4050-213 Porto
  • More: mercado48.pt

Tiles: Prometeu Artesanato

For azulejos to take home, Prometeu Artesanato sells patterned tiles (they can personalise them) along with other Portuguese crafts, from several shops in the centre.

  • Where: Rua de Mouzinho da Silveira 136 and Rua de São João 19, among others; the full list is on prometeuartesanato.com

Make Your Own: Tile and Ceramic Workshops

If you'd rather make your souvenir yourself, Domus Arte runs two-hour beginner workshops where you paint your own azulejo and take it home, at its shops in Sé (Rua da Bainharia) and Lapa (Rua de Antero de Quental). The Lapa shop also does ceramic painting.

doBARRO is a ceramics studio and shop that sells handmade pieces and prints and runs workshops for all levels. When I checked, a freestyle ceramics session was €50 and tile painting €45.

  • doBARRO: Rua da Alegria 246, Porto. Workshop dates and prices on dobarro.art

Dressing the Part: Where to Find Porto's Best Vintage

The city's vintage scene has grown a lot. If you're hunting for a 70s leather jacket or a broken-in pair of jeans, these are the shops I'd start with.

The Art Block: Rua de Miguel Bombarda

Porto's "Art Block," Rua de Miguel Bombarda, is the natural home for all things alternative. It's best known for its contemporary art galleries, but the street and the area around it also have plenty of vintage and second-hand shops. Step into the Centro Comercial Bombarda (CCB), an alternative shopping centre that feels like a creative labyrinth. Inside, you'll find a cluster of independent stores selling fashion, jewellery and design.

  • Where: Rua de Miguel Bombarda, 4050-124 Porto (CCB is at number 285)

Vintage Shops

  • Mão Esquerda Vintage: well-kept pieces for men and women, especially strong on Asian vintage from the 1940s to the 1980s. (Rua da Alegria 5)
  • Mon Père Vintage: colourful and well organised, from cowboy boots to patterned shirts. (Largo de Alberto Pimentel 38)
  • Chiclete Shop: 90s and 2000s streetwear, denim and cowboy shirts. Listings give more than one address, so check its Instagram for the current shop before you go.
  • Wild at Heart: a small shop with a seasonal selection, from 80s sportswear to trench coats. (Rua do Almada 473)
  • Yeahllow Store: authenticated high-end vintage from the 1950s to the 1990s. Check its opening times before you make the trip out to Boavista. (Edifício do Infante, Avenida da Boavista 3769, shop 10)

Bargains by the Kilo

At O'Kilo you pay for clothes by weight. It takes some digging, but this is where the real bargains are. Com Sotaque is another good stop for second-hand clothes in the same part of town, and Humana, a second-hand chain run by an organisation that collects used clothing, sells more recent clothes and shoes cheaply.

  • O'Kilo: Rua de Santa Catarina 601
  • Com Sotaque: Rua do Alferes Malheiro 118-120
  • Humana: Rua de Passos Manuel 218, among other Porto shops (humana-portugal.org)

Carry a bit of cash. Card payments are common, but many market stalls and some small independent shops still prefer cash.

My final piece of advice? Leave some extra room in your suitcase before you come. Trust me, you're going to need it.
