You've probably already got the Ribeira, Livraria Lello and the Clérigos Tower on your list. Go, they're worth it. This guide is for the rest of the time: the viewpoints, tascas and odd corners that locals use and that most visitors walk straight past.

Views Without the Crowds

Porto is built on hills, so good views are easy to find. Finding one where you can sit down and hear yourself think takes a bit more effort. These are the ones I go back to.

Passeio das Virtudes

A terraced garden facing west over the Douro and the Arrábida Bridge, which makes it the city's favourite sunset spot. On a warm evening the grass fills up with students, families and couples, most of them holding a fino (a small draught beer) from one of the cafés nearby. It's free and it's public, so just turn up about an hour before sunset.

  • Where: Passeio das Virtudes, 4050-629 Porto

Miradouro da Vitória

This viewpoint sits on private land that the owners leave open to the public, which is why so many people walk past without realising it's there. The view takes in the Ribeira rooftops, the Sé, the Dom Luís I Bridge and the port lodges in Gaia. It isn't landscaped, the ground is uneven and it's poorly lit at night, so watch your step. Entry is free.

  • Where: Rua de São Bento da Vitória 11, Porto

Jardim do Morro

Technically this is in Vila Nova de Gaia, at the far end of the upper deck of the Dom Luís I Bridge. Most people cross the bridge, take a photo and turn back. Keep walking and you reach this sloping garden with the classic view of the Ribeira across the river. Come at dusk and watch the lights come on in Porto. There are often street musicians.

  • Where: Avenida da República, 4430-148 Vila Nova de Gaia

Passeio das Fontainhas

A quiet promenade along the hillside east of the Ribeira, with clear views of the Douro bridges and far fewer people than the Cais da Ribeira below. Good for a slow walk when the riverfront is packed.

  • Where: Passeio das Fontainhas, 4000-238 Porto

Igreja de São Lourenço (Grilos)

A short walk from the Sé, this church is part of the Museu de Arte Sacra e Arqueologia of the Seminário Maior. The visit includes a walkway between the two bell towers, with a view over the river and both cities. It's a paid visit through the museum, and it's usually quiet.

  • Where: Largo do Colégio, Porto

Where to Eat and Drink

These are places where the food or the setting is the reason to go, not the postcode.

Capela Incomum

A wine bar inside a small 19th-century chapel. The old altar is still there, now with wine bottles around it. The list is mostly Portuguese and there are small plates to go with it. It's a small room, so book if you're going in the evening.

  • Where: Travessa do Carregal 77, 4050-167 Porto
  • When: Opens late afternoon; check capelaincomum.pt for hours and reservations before you go

Casa Guedes

Casa Guedes has been making its sandes de pernil (slow-roasted pork leg in a crusty roll) since 1987. Order it with melted Serra da Estrela cheese. The original is on Praça dos Poveiros and it's loud and busy. There are now several Casa Guedes in Porto and Gaia, all open every day.

  • Where: Praça dos Poveiros 130, 4000-393 Porto

Taberna dos Mercadores

A tiny stone-walled tavern on a narrow street in the Ribeira, with only a few tables. The cooking is traditional Portuguese with a lot of fish and seafood. You need a reservation, and they only take bookings by phone in a one-hour window each morning.

  • Where: Rua dos Mercadores 36-38, 4050-373 Porto
  • When: Tuesday to Sunday, 12:30 to 22:30; closed Mondays
  • Booking: Phone +351 222 010 510 between 10:30 and 11:30

Cervejaria Gazela

Ask anyone from Porto where to get a cachorrinho and they'll send you here. It's a thin, crispy toasted hot dog with cheese and a spicy sauce, cut into pieces. Sit at the counter and watch them make it.

  • Where: Travessa de Cimo de Vila 4, 4000-434 Porto (near Batalha)
  • When: Monday to Saturday, 12:00 to 22:30; see cervejariagazela.pt

Leitaria da Quinta do Paço

This dairy shop goes back to 1920 and is famous for its éclairs, filled with whipped cream instead of custard. The chocolate one is the classic, but they make other flavours too.

  • Where: Praça Guilherme Gomes Fernandes 47, 4050-293 Porto

Mercado Bom Sucesso

Everyone will tell you to visit the Mercado do Bolhão, and you should. For lunch or a casual dinner, though, I often end up at Bom Sucesso in Boavista. It's a renovated market hall that is now mostly food stalls, so it works well for a group where everyone wants something different.

  • Where: Praça do Bom Sucesso 74-90, 4150-145 Porto
  • When: Every day; stall hours vary, see mercadobomsucesso.pt

Maus Hábitos

Yes, the entrance is through a car park building. Go up to the 4th floor and you'll find a restaurant, bar, gallery and concert space in one. It's known for its pizzas, and on most nights there's a gig or a DJ.

  • Where: Rua de Passos Manuel 178, 4º piso, 4000-382 Porto
  • What's on: maushabitos.com

Things to Do Off the Usual Route

Climb the Arrábida Bridge

Since 2016 you can climb the concrete arch of the Arrábida Bridge with a guide, clipped to a safety line the whole way. From the top you see the city, the mouth of the Douro and the Atlantic. The visit itself lasts about 40 minutes; allow an hour with the safety briefing.

  • Where: Rua do Ouro 680, Porto (entrance from the riverside road, up the granite steps about 50 metres west of the bridge)
  • Price: €20 per person; €14 for residents of Portugal
  • Booking: Online at portobridgeclimb.com, by 9:00 on the day of your visit

Museu do Carro Eléctrico

Porto's tram museum is in an old thermoelectric power station on the riverfront at Massarelos. The collection runs from horse-drawn trams to the electric cars that still run on line 1. Afterwards, catch line 1 outside and ride along the river to Foz.

  • Where: Alameda de Basílio Teles 51, 4150-127 Porto
  • When: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00
  • Price: €8 adults, €4 for ages 6 to 18, students and over-65s; see museudocarroelectrico.pt

Centro Português de Fotografia

The national photography centre is in the old Cadeia da Relação, an 18th-century prison. You walk through former cells to see the exhibitions and a big collection of old cameras. Entry is free.

  • Where: Largo Amor de Perdição, 4050-008 Porto
  • When: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; see cpf.pt

Serralves Park

Most people come to Serralves for the contemporary art museum, but the park around it is worth a visit by itself: formal gardens, woodland, a farm and a treetop walk. You can buy a park-only ticket.

  • Where: Rua Dom João de Castro 210, 4150-417 Porto
  • Price: Park only €15 (€12 for residents of Portugal at the ticket office); all spaces €24. See serralves.pt

Casa da Música

Rem Koolhaas's concert hall is no secret, but most visitors photograph the outside and leave. The guided tour takes you through the halls and explains how the building works. Better still, buy a ticket for a concert.

  • Where: Avenida da Boavista 604-610, 4149-071 Porto
  • Tours: €12. Tours in Portuguese, Spanish, French and Italian are at 11:00 and 15:00 (tickets at the box office only); check English tour times at casadamusica.com

Jardins do Palácio de Cristal

Gardens on a bluff above the Douro, with fountains, shady paths and peacocks wandering about. The terraces at the far end look down over the river towards Gaia and the sea.

  • Where: Rua de Dom Manuel II, Porto

Foz do Douro

Take tram 1 or a bus west to Foz, where the Douro meets the Atlantic. Walk the promenade out to the Felgueiras lighthouse and stop for a coffee on the way. It's at its best on a windy day when the waves break over the pier.

Half Rabbit by Bordalo II

On a street corner in Vila Nova de Gaia there's a giant rabbit coming out of a wall, built by the artist Bordalo II from rubbish: burnt metal, car parts and plastic collected around the city. It's visible from the street at any time.

  • Where: Rua Guilherme Gomes Fernandes, Vila Nova de Gaia

Small Places to Find on Foot

  • Largo da Pena Ventosa: A tiny square just below the Sé, with narrow, mismatched houses and laundry on the balconies. People live here, so keep it quiet.
  • Escadas dos Guindais: Old stairs near the base of the Dom Luís I Bridge on the Porto side. You get a view up at the iron structure that you don't get from the decks.

Last tip: wear proper shoes. The cobbles are steep and slippery when it rains, and the best of these places are only reachable on foot.
