Baris here. Porto sits on the Atlantic, and the beaches start where the Douro meets the sea at Foz. North is Matosinhos, the surf beach you can reach by metro. South, in Vila Nova de Gaia, the coast is rockier and the beaches are smaller. Further out, Vila do Conde and Espinho make easy day trips by metro or train. The water is cold even in August, and the waves can be strong, so swim where there are lifeguards.

Matosinhos: Where Porto Learns to Surf

Take metro line A (blue) to Matosinhos Sul and you are a few minutes' walk from the sand. Praia de Matosinhos is long and wide with a sandy bottom, and the waves are usually manageable, which is why most of the city's surf schools are here. The end nearest the port breakwater tends to be calmer for swimming.

Where: Avenida General Norton de Matos, Matosinhos.

Surf Schools

These schools all have listings and opening hours on Google Maps. Lesson prices and rental rates change by season, so ask the school directly.

  • Onda Pura Surf Center: group and private lessons on Praia Moderna. Where: Avenida General Norton de Matos, 4450-295 Matosinhos.
  • Fish Surf School: Where: Praceta Manuel Carlos Seabra Monteiro 13, Matosinhos.
  • Linha de Onda Surfing School: Where: Rua Brito e Cunha 896, 4450-208 Matosinhos.
  • Surfaventura: Where: Avenida General Norton de Matos 1177, 4450-208 Matosinhos.

Ask whether the board and wetsuit are included before you book.

Lunch After the Beach

Matosinhos is where Porto goes for grilled fish. On Rua Heróis de França and the streets around it, the grills are often outside on the pavement.

  • O Gaveto: a long-running seafood restaurant. Where: Rua Roberto Ivens 826, 4450-279 Matosinhos. Closed Tuesdays.
  • Salta o Muro: simple, good grilled fish. Where: Rua Heróis de França 386, 4450-155 Matosinhos. Tuesday to Saturday, 12:00 to 15:00 and 19:00 to 22:45; closed Sundays and Mondays.

Leça da Palmeira and the Piscina das Marés

Across the port from Matosinhos, Leça da Palmeira has a Blue Flag beach (2026) and the Piscina das Marés, the saltwater pools Álvaro Siza built into the rocks in the 1960s. They are a national monument and a good choice if the sea is too rough. The pools only open in the bathing season, daily from 9:00 to 19:00, and entry is paid. Check dates and prices with MatosinhosSport before you go.

The Beaches of Foz

Between the river mouth and Matosinhos, Foz do Douro has a string of small beaches between the rocks along the promenade, including Praia dos Ingleses, Praia do Homem do Leme and Praia de Gondarém. They are better for a dip and a coffee on the seafront than for long swims, and conditions change quickly, so watch the flags. STCP bus 500 runs here from the centre, and historic tram line 1 goes along the river to Passeio Alegre.

Vila Nova de Gaia: Rocks, Dunes and a Chapel

South of the river, the Gaia coast is a run of smaller beaches linked by a boardwalk, with rocks in the water and dunes behind. Gaia had 19 Blue Flag beaches in 2026. The CP urban trains on the Aveiro line from São Bento stop at Francelos, Miramar, Aguda and Granja, all close to the sand.

Praia do Senhor da Pedra

The beach with the small chapel, the Capela do Senhor da Pedra, built on a rock right on the sand. At high tide the sea surrounds it; at low tide you can walk up to it. Blue Flag in 2026. Mind the rocks in the water when you swim.

Where: Gulpilhares, Vila Nova de Gaia. Nearest train stops: Miramar or Francelos.

Praia de Miramar

A short walk along the boardwalk from Senhor da Pedra, with the same wide sand and rocky patches. Also a Blue Flag beach in 2026, and a good choice for a full beach day.

Where: Miramar, Vila Nova de Gaia. Train stop: Miramar.

Day Trips Along the Coast

Vila do Conde

North of Porto at the end of metro line B, Vila do Conde has a long urban seafront. In 2026 its Blue Flag beaches were Frente Urbana Norte, Frente Urbana Sul, Labruge, Mindelo and Vila Chã.

Espinho

South of Gaia, Espinho is a seaside town with a long straight beach and bigger, more powerful waves, better for experienced surfers than beginners. CP urban trains from São Bento take about 35 to 40 minutes. Espinho-Rua 37, Paramos and Seca were Blue Flag beaches in 2026.

Lifeguards and the Bathing Season

Lifeguards are on duty during the official bathing season (época balnear), which runs roughly from June to September. Dates are set beach by beach each year, so check the APA bathing season page or the local council before you go. Outside the season, beaches are unsupervised. Always swim between the flags. Blue Flag awards are renewed every year: the current list is on bandeiraazul.abaae.pt.
