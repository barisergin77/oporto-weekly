Hello, fellow explorers! Baris here. One of the questions I get asked most often is, "Beyond the wine and the river, what is there to see in Porto?" My answer is usually art. In one afternoon you can go from a museum founded in 1833 to a rabbit made of rubbish, and in the evening choose between a play at a national theatre and a concert in a Rem Koolhaas building.

So, grab a coffee and let me walk you through it: the galleries, the museums, the street art, the stages and the festivals worth planning a trip around.

The Gallery Street: Rua de Miguel Bombarda

If you want to see what Porto's contemporary art scene is doing, start on Rua de Miguel Bombarda. Locals call it the "Art Block". The street and the ones around it are packed with galleries, concept stores and artist studios. The best time to go is during the Inaugurações Simultâneas (Simultaneous Openings): every couple of months, on a Saturday, the galleries open their new shows at the same time and the street fills with people. Dates are announced on the Inaugurações Simultâneas Facebook page.

Galleries on the Art Block

Even without an opening, a walk down the street is worth it. The commercial galleries are free to walk into. A few to start with:

  • Galeria Fernando Santos: the first gallery on the street, opened in 1993 in a former wine warehouse, and now one of the biggest in the city. It shows Portuguese names like Pedro Cabrita Reis alongside international artists. (Rua de Miguel Bombarda 526)
  • Galeria Quadrado Azul: one of the city's oldest galleries, founded in 1986. It shows established artists next to the younger ones it represents. (Rua de Miguel Bombarda)
  • Galeria Presença: another long-standing gallery on the street, with a good eye for new talent. (Rua de Miguel Bombarda 570)
  • Tilsitt Gallery: opened in 2020 and now one of the larger commercial spaces in the quarter, with a deliberately mixed programme of contemporary work. (Rua de Miguel Bombarda 171)
  • Ó! Galeria: a small gallery for illustration, with walls covered in framed drawings and shelves of prints, zines and books. A print from here makes a far better souvenir than a fridge magnet. (Rua de Miguel Bombarda 61)

A short walk away, the Galeria Municipal do Porto sits in the gardens of the Palácio de Cristal and runs a changing programme of contemporary art exhibitions. Admission is free. (Jardins do Palácio de Cristal, Rua de D. Manuel II; Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00)

Porto's Museums

The galleries show what's new. The museums hold the collections, and two of them are reason enough to come.

Serralves

You can't talk about art in Porto without Serralves. The Museum of Contemporary Art is a white, minimalist building by Álvaro Siza Vieira, Porto's Pritzker Prize-winning architect, and the same ticket covers the park, the treetop walk, the Art Deco Casa de Serralves, the Álvaro Siza wing and the Casa do Cinema Manoel de Oliveira. Give it half a day.

  • Tickets: €24 for all spaces, €15 for the park only. Half price for ages 12 to 17, students up to 25 and over-65s; free for under-12s. Residents of Portugal pay €20 at the ticket desk and get in free on the first Sunday of the month. Prices on serralves.pt
  • Where: Rua D. João de Castro 210

Museu Nacional Soares dos Reis

For Portuguese art, go to the Soares dos Reis National Museum. It began in 1833 as the country's first public art museum, and since 1940 it has been in the Carrancas Palace, built at the end of the 18th century. The collection is strongest on Portuguese painting and sculpture, particularly from the 19th century, and includes O Desterrado, the best-known sculpture by António Soares dos Reis himself. It's a quiet museum, which is part of the appeal.

  • When: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00
  • Tickets: €10. Details on Museus e Monumentos de Portugal
  • Where: Rua de D. Manuel II 44

World of Wine (WOW)

Across the river in Vila Nova de Gaia, World of Wine is a cultural quarter with five museums. Wine is the main theme (this is Porto, after all), but there's also The Chocolate Experience, a museum of the history of the Porto region and The Art of Drinking, with more than 2,000 objects from 9,000 years of drinking rituals. The Day Pass covers all five, with tastings included in the wine and chocolate museums. Check the current price on wow.pt. (Rua do Choupelo 39, Vila Nova de Gaia)

Street Art

Over the last decade the city has filled up with murals. Keep your eyes open as you walk through Miragaia, the Baixa and Vila Nova de Gaia.

Murals and Where to Find Them

  • "Half Rabbit" by Bordalo II: a giant rabbit built into the corner of a building from rubbish and recycled materials (steel, cans, wood, old tyres), half of it painted, half left raw. Bordalo II is one of Portugal's best-known street artists, and the piece is about waste. (Rua Guilherme Gomes Fernandes, Vila Nova de Gaia)
  • "Look at Porto" by Vhils: Vhils carves portraits into walls by chipping away layers of plaster and paint. This was his first public piece in Porto, made in 2016. (Miragaia, near the Igreja Paroquial de São Pedro de Miragaia)
  • "Mira" by Daniel Eime: a large stencil portrait of a local resident, painted in 2015. (Largo de Artur Arcos, Miragaia, just off Rua Nova da Alfândega)
  • Mural da Trindade by Hazul and Mr. Dheo: a long mural by two Porto artists, painted in 2014. The man at its centre is Mr. Dheo's father, holding a small model of the Clérigos tower. (Next to Trindade metro station)
  • Liqen's cat: the Spanish artist Liqen painted a huge blue cat peering out of a narrow alley a few blocks from São Bento station. (Rua de Afonso Martins Alho)

Theatre and Music

When the sun sets, Porto's stages light up. Three venues to know:

  • Teatro Nacional São João: the national theatre, in a historic building on Praça da Batalha, with classic and contemporary plays. Popular shows sell out, so book ahead on the theatre's website. (Praça da Batalha)
  • Casa da Música: the concert hall designed by Rem Koolhaas, with a programme from symphony orchestras to jazz and electronic music. (Avenida da Boavista 604-610)
  • Coliseu Porto Ageas: a big historic hall for touring bands, ballet, musicals and comedy. (Rua de Passos Manuel 137)

Festivals Worth Planning Around

  • São João: on the night of 23 June the whole city is out on the streets for Saint John. Expect grilled sardines, street concerts, people tapping each other on the head with plastic hammers and fireworks over the Douro at midnight.
  • Fantasporto: Porto's international film festival, strong on fantasy and horror, runs from late February into March at the Batalha Centro de Cinema. The next edition is 26 February to 7 March 2027 (fantasporto.com).
  • Primavera Sound Porto: four days of indie, rock and electronic music in the Parque da Cidade every June. The 2027 edition is 10 to 13 June (primaverasound.com).
  • Essência do Vinho: a big wine fair in the Palácio da Bolsa every February, with more than 4,000 wines on show at the last edition. The next Porto edition is 18 to 21 February 2027; ticket prices are on essenciadovinho.com.

My best advice? Wear comfortable shoes. Bombarda, Miragaia and the Baixa are all walkable from one another, and a lot of the murals above you'll only find on foot.
