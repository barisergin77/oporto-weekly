Hello everyone, Baris here. Porto food is heavy, meaty and cheap, and most of the classics are sandwiches or stews. Below are the five dishes I'd tell any visitor to try, with the places I'd send you for each, then fish, tascas, a few sit-down restaurants and the markets.

Five Dishes and Where to Eat Them

Most of these places are in the Baixa, within a 15-minute walk of each other. Many close on Sundays, so plan the big eating days for the rest of the week.

Francesinha

Ham, linguiça sausage and steak between slices of bread, covered in melted cheese and a spiced tomato and beer sauce, usually with a fried egg on top and chips on the side. It's a knife-and-fork meal, and one is plenty for lunch.

  • Café Santiago: the name most locals give you first, and there's often a queue. Monday to Saturday 12:00 to 22:45, closed Sunday (per cafesantiago.pt).
    Where: Rua de Passos Manuel 226, 4000-382 Porto
  • Brasão: a beer hall with a well-made francesinha and craft beer. There are two in the Baixa; lunch and dinner hours differ by branch, see brasao.pt.
    Where: Rua de Ramalho Ortigão 28 (Aliados) and Rua de Passos Manuel 205 (Coliseu)
  • A Regaleira: the restaurant that claims to have served the first francesinha, in the 1950s.
    Where: Rua do Bonjardim 83, 4000-124 Porto

Tripas à Moda do Porto

The dish that gave the people of Porto their nickname, tripeiros. It's a stew of tripe, white beans, sausage and other meats. The story goes that in the 15th century the city gave its best meat to the ships of Henry the Navigator and kept the offal for itself.

  • Tripeiro: open on this street since 1952, now run by new owners, with tripas as the house speciality.
    Where: Rua de Passos Manuel 195, 4000-385 Porto
  • O Buraco: a small, plain tavern near Bolhão serving traditional home cooking, tripas included.
    Where: Rua do Bolhão 95, 4000-112 Porto

Sandes de Pernil

Slow-roasted pork leg, sliced into a crusty roll so the juices soak into the bread. Ask for it with Serra da Estrela cheese melted on top.

  • Casa Guedes: a busy, no-frills snack bar with the pork roasting in the window. Have it with a cold beer.
    Where: Praça dos Poveiros 130, Porto
  • Antunes: if you'd rather sit down to roast pork shank (pernil assado) on a plate, with potatoes. Closed Sunday.
    Where: Rua do Bonjardim 614, 4000-124 Porto

Cachorrinho

A spicy sausage in thin, crisp bread with cheese, pressed in a grill until it all melts, then cut into bite-sized pieces and served with hot sauce.

  • Gazela Cachorrinhos da Batalha: the narrow bar with the half-moon counter that Anthony Bourdain made famous abroad. Order a cachorrinho and a Super Bock at the counter. Monday to Saturday 12:00 to 22:30 (per cervejariagazela.pt). There's a second, roomier Gazela round the corner on Rua de Entreparedes 34.
    Where: Travessa de Cimo de Vila 4, 4000-434 Porto

Pastel de Nata

The custard tart comes from Lisbon, but Porto has plenty of good ones. Eat it warm, with cinnamon.

  • Manteigaria: they bake in front of you and serve the tarts warm. Porto has four: by Bolhão market, on Rua de Santa Catarina, at Clérigos and on Largo de São Domingos (see manteigaria.com).
    Where (Bolhão): Rua de Alexandre Braga 24, 4000-049 Porto

For more nata and coffee stops, see our guide to Porto's best coffee and pastéis de nata.

More to Order

  • Bifana: thin slices of pork marinated in garlic and white wine, in a bread roll. Conga on Rua do Bonjardim 318 has been doing them for decades; closed Sunday.
  • Bacalhau à Gomes de Sá: a Porto way with salt cod, baked with potatoes and onion and topped with boiled egg and olives. Abadia do Porto (Rua do Ateneu Comercial do Porto 22, closed Sunday) is a big traditional dining room that serves it.
  • Caldo verde: potato and shredded kale soup, usually with a slice of chouriço. It's on most traditional menus.
  • Polvo à lagareiro: octopus roasted with lots of garlic and olive oil, with small "punched" potatoes.

Fish and Seafood

For grilled fish, go to Matosinhos, the fishing town just north of the city (metro line A). On and around Rua Heróis de França the restaurants grill on charcoal out on the street.

  • O Lusitano: a plain, traditional place. Pick your fish from the display and they grill it.
    Where: Rua Heróis de França 329, 4450-158 Matosinhos
  • Taberna dos Mercadores: a tiny tavern in Ribeira known for its fish. It has very few tables, so book ahead.
    Where: Rua dos Mercadores 36, Porto

Tascas

Tascas are small, often family-run taverns with home-style food and little decor.

Adega São Nicolau

A Ribeira favourite with straightforward Portuguese cooking and tables out on the side street. Monday to Saturday, lunch and dinner, closed Sunday. It's popular, so book by phone.

Where: Rua de São Nicolau 1, 4050-561 Porto

Escondidinho do Barredo

A tiny tavern serving petiscos (small plates) from a single hand-written menu that gets passed between tables. Order a few things to share, like the fried sardines or the iscas de bacalhau (salt cod fritters). Hours are short and it closes some weekdays, so check its Google Maps listing first.

Where: Rua dos Canastreiros 28, Porto

O Ernesto

A small, family-style restaurant with daily specials. Lunch Monday to Saturday, dinner Tuesday to Saturday, closed Sunday.

Where: Rua da Picaria 85, Porto

A Few Sit-Down Restaurants

  • Cantinho do Avillez: José Avillez's relaxed restaurant with modern Portuguese dishes, open every day.
    Where: Rua de Mouzinho da Silveira 166, 4050-416 Porto
  • Pedro Lemos: one Michelin star, tasting menus, in Foz.
    Where: Rua do Ouro 258, 4150-554 Porto
  • The Yeatman Gastronomic Restaurant: two Michelin stars, at The Yeatman hotel in Gaia, looking over the river to Porto.
    Where: Rua do Choupelo, 4400-088 Vila Nova de Gaia

Food Markets

Mercado do Bolhão

The city's historic market, reopened after a long restoration. Fresh fish, fruit and veg, meat, cheese and flowers, with small restaurants upstairs. Market Monday to Friday 8:00 to 19:00 and Saturday 8:00 to 18:00; restaurants Monday to Saturday 8:00 to midnight; closed Sundays and public holidays (per mercadobolhao.pt).

Where: Rua Formosa 322, 4000-248 Porto

Mercado Bom Sucesso

A modern market hall with food stalls, from Portuguese snacks to sushi, good for a group that can't agree. Open every day until 23:00, and until midnight on Fridays and Saturdays (per mercadobomsucesso.pt).

Where: Praça do Bom Sucesso 74-90, 4150-145 Porto

Mercado Beira-Rio

Across the Dom Luís I Bridge on the Gaia riverfront, a handy stop for petiscos after a Port lodge visit. Check mercadobeirario.pt for hours.

Where: Av. de Ramos Pinto 148, 4400-261 Vila Nova de Gaia

One last tip: in Porto an espresso is a cimbalino. Ask for one after lunch and nobody will look at you twice. Bom apetite!
