Hello, Porto lovers! Baris here. One of the things I like most about our city is how much is within a day's reach. Friends and readers ask me for day-trip ideas all the time, so I've put my answers in one place: the Douro Valley for wine, Braga and Guimarães for history, and Aveiro and Costa Nova for canals and sea air. All three work by public transport.

The Douro Valley

Let's start with the big one. The first time you see the terraced vineyards running down to the river, you'll understand why the Alto Douro wine region is a UNESCO World Heritage site. It's also where port comes from.

Getting to the Douro: Three Options

  • A guided tour: the no-stress option. A typical small-group day leaves Porto in the morning, visits two quintas with tastings, stops for lunch and often includes a short boat trip from Pinhão, getting back to the city in the early evening. Prices and what's included vary a lot between companies, so compare a few, and you won't have to worry about driving after the tastings.
  • The train: my favourite, for the views. The Linha do Douro runs from São Bento and Campanhã and follows the river for much of the way. The InterRegional trains that go on to Pinhão take about two and a half hours from São Bento, and a single costs €12.45. Fares are in CP's Porto-Régua-Pocinho price table and times in the Douro line timetable.
  • The car: the most flexible. The N-222 between Peso da Régua and Pinhão follows the river and is a beautiful drive. Just be sure to have a designated driver.

Quintas Worth the Trip

There are hundreds of wine estates (quintas) to choose from. Book ahead, especially from May to October. Here are four that I like:

  • For the view: Quinta do Seixo (Sandeman)
    High on a hill above the river, with one of the best panoramas in the valley. The basic visit is €26: 50 minutes in the cellar and a tasting of three Sandeman ports (white, ruby and tawny). There are longer visits, including one with lunch. Book on Sogrape's wine tourism site. Address: Valença do Douro, 5120-495 Tabuaço.
  • If you come by train: Quinta do Bomfim (Dow's)
    Right in Pinhão, close to the station. The Symington family has farmed it for five generations, and you can see the old lodge built in 1896, then pick from several tastings, from the classic ports to vintage ones. Visits need a reservation on symington.com. The riverside restaurant, Bomfim 1896, is a good place for lunch. Address: Largo do Videira, 5085-060 Pinhão.
  • For something different: Quinta da Pacheca
    Known for its "wine barrel" rooms, giant barrels in the vineyard that you can sleep in. Guided visits with tastings start at €26 (quintadapacheca.com), and there are other experiences on the estate's site. Address: Cambres, Lamego.
  • For a small family estate: Quinta do Tedo
    A family-run quinta where the Tedo river meets the Douro. The tour and tasting (two Douro wines and three ports from the estate) is €36 per person; a tawny tasting from the barrels in their underground cellar is €64 per person, with a minimum of two (quintadotedo.com). Address: N-222, Folgosa.

Braga and Guimarães

If you'd rather swap vineyards for history, Braga and Guimarães make a good pair. Both are on CP's urban train network from São Bento, so you don't need a tour. A single costs a few euros, priced by the number of zones (see CP's urban train fares). Guided day tours that cover both cities in one day also leave from Porto.

Braga

Braga is a city of churches, and its Sé is one of the oldest cathedrals in Portugal. The main reason to come, though, is the Santuário do Bom Jesus do Monte, a hilltop sanctuary with a huge baroque staircase. You can climb the steps, or do what I do: take the water-powered funicular up (it opened in 1882) and walk down. The funicular costs €2.50 one way or €4 return (bomjesus.pt).

Guimarães

A short bus ride from Braga, Guimarães has a medieval centre on the UNESCO World Heritage list, and the old wall with "Aqui nasceu Portugal" ("Portugal was born here") written on it. Guimarães is traditionally said to be where Afonso Henriques, Portugal's first king, was born. Go to the Castelo de Guimarães and, next door, the Paço dos Duques de Bragança, a 15th-century palace.

  • When: Every day, 10:00 to 18:00 (last entry 17:30). The castle keep is closed to visitors.
  • Tickets: Castle €5, palace €10, or €13 for both. Buy them at the palace or online (pacodosduques.gov.pt).

Aveiro and Costa Nova

Had your fill of history and wine? Aveiro and the beach at Costa Nova are the easy seaside option: canals, painted boats and salty air.

Getting There

The urban train from São Bento runs to Aveiro (the Aveiro line is part of CP's Porto urban network, so the same zone fares apply). From the bus terminal by the station, Busway lines 35 and 36 go to Costa Nova (stop: Largo Arrais Ançã). Line 36 takes about 45 minutes through the villages; line 35 is quicker, using the motorway. Times are on busway-cira.pt.

Canals and Striped Houses

In Aveiro, take a trip on a moliceiro, the painted boats once used to collect seaweed. The tour of the city canals takes 45 minutes and costs about €14 for an adult. Before you leave, try ovos moles, the local sweet made from egg yolks and sugar. Then take the bus to Costa Nova, famous for its palheiros: old fishermen's houses painted in bright vertical stripes. Walk the wooden boardwalks and finish on the long Atlantic beach.

Baris's Final Tip

For any of these trips, book ahead. Douro tastings in particular can be full weeks in advance from May to October.
